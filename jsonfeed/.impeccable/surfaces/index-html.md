# The Feline Archive: Collection Drawers

Mode: Experience. Build path for this session: code-first, following the user's previous preference for direct implementation; no project-wide build-path setting is stored.

## Product truth
An independent, live catalogue of cat-related works returned by the Art Institute of Chicago API. Artwork, artist, date and museum source must stay legible. Saved works are local to this browser. The site is not an official museum product.

## Direction
The world is an open print-room cabinet: broad, pale mounts carrying actual artwork; crisp ink labels on adjacent rails; a deep teal index surface; and vermilion marks used sparingly for selection and saved state. It should feel like handling catalogued objects, not scrolling identical cards. The first screen pairs a compact searchable index with visible artworks, including one larger lead work. Opening a work reveals a larger image, full label, adjacent-work navigation, save control, and a direct museum record link.

The cabinet is functional rather than literal. No fake drawers, paper textures, or ornamental hinges. Strong image scale, generous margins around objects, thin catalog rules, and stable accession-style metadata carry the material idea.

## First viewport
The Feline Archive title and museum attribution identify the collection. Search, All/Saved views, result status, and at least two actual artworks are visible without scrolling on desktop. On mobile, the art remains the first meaningful content below compact controls; no empty hero.

## Visitor path
Browse live cat-art search results, refine with a query, load more, open a work, inspect its image and details, save it locally, move to adjacent works, and follow its source record. Empty, loading, failed-fetch and missing-image states have useful next actions. Search changes the result set; saved view remains usable when the live API is unavailable.

## Signature interaction
The selected work expands into a native dialog with a quiet image stage and its catalog label. Previous/next and keyboard arrows navigate without returning to the grid. On close, focus returns to the triggering tile. Motion is a short, reduced-motion-safe reveal.

## Cross-surface reach
The mount, label, rail, index, and selection vocabulary can carry future exhibitions or artist subsets without borrowing the portfolio's main-site chrome.

## Risk
Live API relevance may include works only loosely related to cats, and images can be unavailable. Present this as a search collection, never a complete curated museum show; provide titles, artists, and source links even when media fails.

## Quality bar
Artwork is the main visual asset. Use distinct image proportions and asymmetrical rhythm, not icon cards. Labels stay scannable and truthful, controls are usable by keyboard and touch, and the page remains functional without animation. The visual finish should have the typographic discipline of a printed collection record and the density control of a working archive.

## Challengers considered
Teletext and live ASCII rendering would obscure the artworks, so they lost audience identification and clarity; keep their disciplined indexing and performance restraint. Azulejo panels suit narrative order but not flexible live search; keep whole-unit layout rhythm without tiled ornament. Sneaker archive drawers offered a useful open-state idea but its box costume misidentifies the subject; keep a decisive selected-object state. Information-noise sleeves weaken art legibility; keep only their commitment to a clear hierarchy. Magazine identity supports rich editorial type but is less direct for search; keep its deliberate caption system.
